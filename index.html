Get started

Three steps to the first take. You need ComfyUI v0.40 or newer, an NVIDIA GPU with 8 GB or more, and system RAM for the model file plus about 8 GB.

Install the nodes

Clone into custom_nodes and restart ComfyUI. Nothing to pip install.

cd ComfyUI/custom_nodes
git clone https://github.com/PedroMarinhoDev/ComfyUI-Drama3

Download three files

From Hugging Face:

drama3_fp8experts_bf16.safetensors
  → models/diffusion_models  38.6 GB
drama3_codec.safetensors
  → models/vae                1.6 GB
qwen3_asr_1.7b_bf16.safetensors
  → models/asr                4.1 GB

Open the workflow and queue

Drag drama3_voice_clone.json into ComfyUI, pick a few seconds of clean speech, write your text, run.

Performance

Milliseconds per audio frame as ComfyUI runs the nodes, experts in RAM unless noted, a cloned voice, ~9 s of speech, the mean of two seeds; measured on 2026-10-17 with an Intel Core i9-14900K, against the pack of 2026-10-16 measured the same way. Drama 3 makes 21.5 frames per second of speech: the line marks real time, 46 ms. The held bars ran with the rest of the 3090's memory taken, as on a 12 GB or 8 GB card.

Nothing waits on the CPUThe pack's CUDA kernels look experts up in the VRAM cache on the GPU and read the missed ones straight from RAM, so every layer replays as a CUDA graph. PyTorch compiles them at run time: no compiler or package to install.
The CPU lends a handWith int8 experts the CPU computes part of the experts the cache misses while the GPU runs the rest, which matters most on small cards: twice as fast as FP8 with a 4 GB cache.
Transcripts are quickQwen3-ASR transcribes a 6.6 s clip in ~2 s (1.7B) or ~1 s (0.6B) on an RTX 3090.

FAQ

Which file should I download?

FP8 if you have about 48 GB of free system RAM: it's the original weights. On a card under 24 GB take int8 instead: the same size, measured as close to the original, and twice as fast there because the CPU computes part of its experts. W6A8 needs ~40 GB, W4A8 ~32 GB, and both run fastest on a 24 GB card; W4A8 falls silent more often with real voice clips.

Can I clone any voice?

Only voices you have the right to use. The Fish Audio Community License forbids imitating an identifiable person without their consent.

Why does a take sometimes go silent?

Usually the voice: the transcript must cover every word of the clip, and the clip should be clean speech cut between sentences. Some clips make the model fall silent in Fish Audio's own code too (a known issue); try another seed or another stretch of the recording.

Speech comes out slow or flat

Keep temperature at 1.0 and top_p at 0.9: lower values add pauses and flatten the voice. The model copies the reference's delivery, so pick a lively clip for a lively take.

Which tags can I use?

Any: directions are free natural language, not a fixed list. Point tags such as [sighs] or [pause] land where you put them, <whisper>...</whisper> shapes the words inside, a [style] at the start sets the whole line, and <|speaker:N|> (0 to 15) switches speakers. Fish Audio's full syntax is Appendix C of the Drama 3 technical report; the README has a table.

Can I keep a voice without encoding the clip again?

Yes. Save Drama 3 Voice writes the encoded voice, codes and transcript, to a .safetensors of a few KB in output/drama3_voices; Load Drama 3 Voice brings it back with no clip to load and nothing to encode or transcribe again. The example workflow has the save node, muted.

Do I need a voice at all?

No. Without one the model invents a voice, a different one for each seed; the two voices on this page were made that way.

Can I use it commercially?

Drama 3's weights and outputs fall under the Fish Audio Community License: free for research and non-commercial use, and for commercial use below USD 500k annual revenue; a hosted TTS service needs a license from Fish Audio. Read the license for the details.
